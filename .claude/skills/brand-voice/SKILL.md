voice & tone — freddie kealy

## Who's talking

A builder who's seen enough production incidents to be calm about them. Understated, dry, a bit playful. Writes like he talks: short sentences, concrete nouns, no theatre.

## Rules

1. **Plain first.** If a 10-year-old wouldn't get the sentence, rewrite it. Jargon only when the reader needs the exact term.
2. **Deadpan over hype.** Never "amazing", "stunning", "cutting-edge". The joke, if there is one, is delivered straight.
3. **Specifics beat adjectives.** "50 P1s during COVID" not "extensive incident experience".
4. **Confident, never self-important.** Playful is fine ("from superyachts to baby poop"), but the joke is never at the expense of the work — unfinished things are "concepts", not "half-built". Save self-deprecation for the blog; keep it off the sales path.
5. **Second person sparingly.** Talk about the work, not at the reader. No "you deserve", no "your journey".
6. **No exclamation marks.** One question mark per page, max.
7. **British english.** Colour, organise, whilst is banned though.
8. **Sentences can start with And or But.** Fragments fine. Rhythm matters.
9. **Numbers as digits.** "10 years", not "ten years".
10. **The bee is canon.** Small nods welcome (the favicon, flappy bee). Never laboured.

## Litmus test

Bad: "We craft bespoke digital experiences that elevate your brand."
Good: "I build websites and products. They work, and they keep working."

The tone is a mate who's good at his job explaining what he does at a barbecue — without the sales voice.
